How to Keep a Trading Journal and Review Your Setups

A journal is the only source of evidence about how you actually trade. Done well, it shows which setups work for you, which habits cost you money, and what to change next. Here's how to build one for gold.

Why most journals don’t help

Many traders start a journal, record entries and exits for a few weeks, and stop. The journal doesn’t help because it records what happened but not why, and nobody ever reviews it. A useful journal does three things:

  1. Records your reasoning before the outcome is known.
  2. Separates the quality of the decision from the result.
  3. Feeds a regular review that leads to a specific change.

What to record for every trade

Field Example
Date and session Tuesday, London open
Setup type Sweep and reverse / breakout-retest / OB return
Higher-timeframe context H4 uptrend, price in discount
Thesis One or two sentences, written before entry
Entry, stop, target Prices and the reasoning for each
Risk % of account and position size
News proximity No high-impact data within 2 hours
Volatility state Calm / normal / elevated
Screenshot at entry Chart with your markings
Exit and result In $ and in R (multiples of initial risk)
Plan followed? Yes / No — which rule was broken
Emotional state Calm, rushed, frustrated, overconfident
Decision grade A / B / C (independent of the result)
Lesson One sentence

Recording results in R (for example +2R or −1R) makes trades comparable regardless of position size.

Grade decisions, not outcomes

Trading outcomes contain a lot of randomness. A well-planned trade can lose; an impulsive one can win. If you only judge by profit, you’ll reinforce bad habits that happened to pay and abandon good ones that happened to lose.

A simple grading rubric:

  • A: Setup matched the plan exactly; risk and execution correct.
  • B: Valid setup with a minor execution issue (late entry, slightly wrong size).
  • C: Not a plan setup, or a major rule broken.

Over time, aim to increase the share of A-grade decisions. That’s fully within your control.

Tagging for gold-specific insights

Tags let you slice your data. Useful tags for XAUUSD include:

  • Session: Asia, London open, London midday, overlap, US afternoon.
  • Setup type.
  • News proximity.
  • Trend alignment: with or against the H4 trend.
  • Volatility state.

After 50 or more trades, simple filters often reveal strong patterns — for example, that most of your losses come from trades taken against the H4 trend, or in the US afternoon.

Don’t forget the trades you didn’t take

Record valid setups you skipped, and setups you considered but rejected. This shows whether your hesitation protects you or costs you, and stops your journal from becoming a list of only the trades you felt confident about.

The weekly review

Set a fixed time each week. Keep it to about 30–45 minutes:

  1. Numbers: trades, win rate, average R, total R, rule breaches.
  2. Best and worst decisions: not trades — decisions.
  3. Patterns: anything repeating across tags.
  4. One change: a single, specific adjustment for next week (for example, “no entries after 15:00 London time”).
  5. Plan update: if the change works over several weeks, add it to your trading plan.

Post-trade reviews with others

Reviewing your own trades has a blind spot: you. Sharing a few journal entries — especially losses — with traders who will challenge your reasoning can surface mistakes you’d never see alone. Structured post-trade reviews are a core part of the XAUUSD Global community vision.

Tools

A spreadsheet is enough. Dedicated journaling software can automate imports and statistics, but the value comes from what you write before entering and what you do in the review — not from the tool.

Apply it to your chart

Think it through

Before your next gold trade using this idea, answer these in your journal — ideally before you know the result.

  1. Are you recording the reason for each trade before you know the result?
  2. Do you write down your invalidation and risk, not just entry and exit?
  3. Which mistakes appear more than once in your journal?
  4. Are you journaling losses as carefully as wins?
  5. When is your weekly review, and what one change will come out of it?
