London vs. New York Sessions: What Gold Traders Should Understand

London and New York are where most of gold's daily movement happens — but they behave differently. Understanding those differences helps you choose a trading window that fits your setups and your life.

The trading day in three parts

Spot gold trades almost continuously on weekdays, but activity follows the sun:

  • Asia — Sydney, Tokyo, Singapore and Hong Kong hours. Often quieter, with narrower ranges, though Chinese and Indian demand and Asian-hours headlines can matter.
  • London — the centre of the over-the-counter gold market. Liquidity rises sharply.
  • New York — overlaps with London in its morning and brings most high-impact US data.

Side-by-side comparison

London New York
Conventional hours (local) 08:00–17:00 London 08:00–17:00 New York
Character First major expansion of the day; tests of the Asian range Overlap volume, data-driven moves, afternoon fade
Key scheduled events LBMA Gold Price auctions (10:30 and 15:00), UK/European data US data (often 08:30), Fed decisions, US equity open (09:30)
Common behaviour Breakouts or sweeps of the Asian range; often an early high or low of the day Continuation or reversal of London; sharp reactions to data
Main risks False moves at the open Spreads, slippage and whipsaws around releases

These are tendencies. Any day can break the pattern.

Converting times to your location

Session hours are conventions in local time, so they shift in UTC when clocks change. The UK and US move their clocks on different dates in spring and autumn, so for a few weeks a year the London–New York overlap is an hour different from usual. If you trade from elsewhere — for example India (UTC+5:30, no daylight saving) — your local times for each session will move twice a year. Always check the current offset rather than relying on memory.

What tends to happen in London

London often brings the first real directional move of the day. Common scenarios include a clean breakout of the Asian range, a sweep of one side followed by a reversal, or a quiet morning ahead of major US data. Read the full London-session gold trading guide.

What tends to happen in New York

New York has the highest concentration of scheduled risk. Inflation, employment and Fed events can move gold many dollars within minutes, and initial reactions frequently reverse. After London closes, liquidity often fades and gold can drift or consolidate. Read the full New York-session gold trading guide.

How to choose your window

Ask yourself:

  1. When can you trade alert and uninterrupted? A tired trader at the New York open is a risk in itself.
  2. What does your setup need? Range breakouts and sweeps of the Asian range suit London. Post-news structure trades suit New York.
  3. How do you handle volatility? If fast news reactions make you anxious, London mornings without major data may suit you better.
  4. What does your journal say? Tag every trade by session. After enough trades, the data usually shows clear differences.

Many traders choose one primary window and specialise in it — learning its rhythm deeply rather than trying to trade all day. Write your choice into your trading plan.

One market, many time zones

Because gold trades around the clock, a global community naturally covers every session: someone is always watching the open you’re asleep for. Comparing notes across sessions — “what did London do with the Asian range?” or “how did New York treat the London high?” — is one of the most practical benefits of learning alongside traders in other time zones.

Apply it to your chart

Think it through

Before your next gold trade using this idea, answer these in your journal — ideally before you know the result.

  1. Which session suits your schedule and your strategy?
  2. What usually happens in that session on days with major US data?
  3. How do you avoid chasing a move the earlier session already made?
  4. Do you change your size or stop width between sessions?
  5. Review: in which session do you trade best — and does your journal agree?

A well-reasoned trade can still lose. The goal is a decision you can explain and learn from. How to keep a trading journal
