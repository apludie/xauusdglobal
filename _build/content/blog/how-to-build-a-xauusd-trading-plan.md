## Why a written plan matters

Most trading decisions that go badly aren't made because a trader lacked knowledge. They're made in the moment: a fast candle, fear of missing out, frustration after a loss. A written plan moves the important decisions to a calm moment — before the market opens — so that during the session you're executing rules rather than inventing them.

A plan also makes improvement possible. If your rules are written down, your [trading journal](/blog/how-to-keep-a-trading-journal/) can show whether the rules failed or whether you failed to follow them. Those are very different problems.

## Section 1: Purpose and constraints

Write down, honestly:

- Why you're trading and what you want to achieve over the next 6–12 months (skill goals, not just money).
- How much time you can realistically spend, and when.
- The capital you are trading — money you can afford to lose.

## Section 2: Market and sessions

- **Market:** XAUUSD only (or list any others).
- **Sessions:** Which hours you'll trade — for example, London open to midday London time. See [London vs. New York sessions](/blog/london-vs-new-york-sessions/).
- **Timeframes:** Bias timeframe, setup timeframe and execution timeframe (for example H4 / H1 / M15).

## Section 3: Setups

List **one to three** setups, each defined precisely enough that another trader could identify it from your description. For each:

- **Context:** required higher-timeframe conditions.
- **Location:** where on the chart the setup must form.
- **Trigger:** the exact event that allows entry.
- **Invalidation:** where the idea is wrong.
- **Management:** targets, partials, trailing rules.

Use our [strategy hub](/xauusd-trading-strategies/) for ideas — but write rules in your own words.

:::hypo
**Setup A — London sweep and reverse (example only).** Context: H4 trend up. Location: price sweeps below the Asian low or previous-day low between 08:00 and 10:00 London time. Trigger: M15 close back inside the range followed by a break of the most recent M15 lower high. Invalidation: below the sweep low plus a buffer. Management: first objective at the Asian high; move stop to break-even only after a new M15 higher low forms.
:::

## Section 4: Risk rules

- Risk per trade (as a percentage of the account).
- Position sizing method — always from the stop distance. See [XAUUSD risk management](/strategies/xauusd-risk-management/).
- Maximum number of trades per day.
- Daily loss limit and weekly loss limit.
- Maximum total open risk.
- What you'll do after a losing streak (for example: halve size after three consecutive losses).

## Section 5: News policy

Gold reacts hard to US data and Fed events. Decide:

- Whether you'll hold positions through high-impact releases.
- How many minutes before and after a release you won't enter.
- Whether you trade at all on Fed decision days.

## Section 6: Routines

- **Pre-session:** calendar check, level mapping, bias, scenarios.
- **During session:** checklist before each entry; screenshot at entry.
- **Post-session:** journal entries completed, screenshots saved.
- **Weekly:** review statistics, rule breaches and one improvement to test next week.

## Section 7: Rules about you

Many plans fail on psychology, not analysis. Add rules such as:

- Stop trading for the day after breaking any plan rule.
- No trading when tired, ill or emotionally upset.
- No increasing size to recover losses.

## A copyable template

```
MARKET: XAUUSD      SESSIONS: ____      TIMEFRAMES: Bias __ / Setup __ / Entry __
SETUP 1: Context ____ | Location ____ | Trigger ____ | Invalidation ____ | Management ____
SETUP 2: ...
RISK: __% per trade | max __ trades/day | daily stop __% | weekly stop __% | max open risk __%
NEWS: No entries __ min before / __ min after high-impact releases. Hold through news? Y/N
ROUTINES: Pre-session ____ | Post-session ____ | Weekly review (day/time) ____
PERSONAL RULES: ____
REVIEW DATE FOR THIS PLAN: ____
```

## Keep it alive

A plan isn't fixed forever. Review it on a set date — monthly works for many traders. Change one thing at a time, based on journal evidence, not on one bad day. And consider sharing it: explaining your plan to another trader is one of the quickest ways to find the parts that are vague.
