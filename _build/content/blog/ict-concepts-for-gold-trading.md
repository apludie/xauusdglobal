## Three questions behind the jargon

ICT concepts — popularised by the educator known as the Inner Circle Trader — include dozens of terms. For gold traders, most of them serve to answer three questions:

1. **Where is price likely to be drawn?** → *liquidity*
2. **What shows that the immediate direction has changed?** → *market structure*
3. **Where can I define entry and risk precisely?** → *imbalances such as fair value gaps*

A fourth element, **time**, filters when those answers are considered meaningful. For the full model with a worked example, see our [ICT trading guide for XAUUSD](/strategies/ict-trading-xauusd/).

## 1. Liquidity: where price may be drawn

ICT practitioners think of obvious highs and lows as pools of orders. Typical gold examples:

- Previous day's and previous week's high and low
- The Asian-session high and low
- Equal highs and equal lows on H1 or H4

The **draw on liquidity** is the pool price seems most likely to reach next, judged from the higher-timeframe structure. A trader expecting price to reach the previous week's high, for example, looks for long setups — not shorts.

When price runs through a pool and reverses, that's a **liquidity sweep** — see [liquidity sweeps on XAUUSD](/strategies/liquidity-sweep-xauusd/).

## 2. Market structure: what shows a shift

After liquidity is taken, ICT traders look for a **market structure shift** — a break of the most recent short-term swing in the opposite direction, typically with strong, fast candles ("displacement"). This is essentially the same idea as a Change of Character, covered in our [market structure guide](/strategies/market-structure-xauusd/).

The shift answers: "Has the move through liquidity actually failed, or is it a breakout?" Without it, there's no evidence of a reversal.

## 3. Fair value gaps: where to define risk

Displacement usually leaves a **fair value gap** — a three-candle imbalance. ICT traders often look to enter when price retraces into that gap, especially where it overlaps the 62–79% "optimal trade entry" retracement of the move. Read our full guide to [fair value gaps on XAUUSD](/strategies/fair-value-gap-xauusd/).

## 4. Time: when it matters

ICT emphasises specific windows — commonly the London open and the New York open — as times when meaningful moves are more likely. For gold, US data releases around 08:30 New York time add a strong timing element. Setups outside these windows are generally treated with more caution.

## How the pieces fit: a simple sequence

1. Higher timeframe: identify the draw on liquidity.
2. Session window: wait for London or New York.
3. Liquidity taken: price sweeps a nearby high or low against the draw.
4. Shift: displacement breaks short-term structure toward the draw.
5. Entry area: the fair value gap left by that displacement.
6. Invalidation: beyond the sweep extreme.
7. Objective: the draw on liquidity.

That's the backbone. Most other ICT terms refine one of these steps.

## A realistic learning roadmap

- **Weeks 1–2:** Market structure only. Label swings, BOS and CHoCH on historical gold charts until your labelling is consistent.
- **Weeks 3–4:** Add liquidity. Before each session, mark pools and record which were taken.
- **Weeks 5–6:** Add FVGs. Record which gaps formed with displacement and which were revisited.
- **Weeks 7–8:** Combine into the sequence above and backtest one session window only.
- **Ongoing:** Journal every setup you'd have taken, including those you skipped.

The schedule is illustrative — move at whatever pace lets your definitions become precise.

## Common pitfalls

- **Concept overload.** Learning every term before mastering the basics makes analysis slower and less consistent.
- **One-minute obsession.** Very low timeframes produce constant shifts and gaps — mostly noise.
- **Ignoring the calendar.** A clean setup minutes before US inflation data isn't clean.
- **Narrative over evidence.** The story about institutions is unverifiable. Judge the approach by your tested results, not by how convincing the explanation sounds.
- **Tight stops in volatile windows.** The windows ICT emphasises are exactly when gold's spreads and slippage are worst.

ICT concepts can help gold traders become precise about *where*, *when* and *how much risk*. The traders who benefit most are usually those who strip the framework down to a few clearly defined rules and test them honestly — ideally alongside others who will challenge their markings.
