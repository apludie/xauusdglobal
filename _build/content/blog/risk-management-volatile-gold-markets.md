## Volatility changes the maths

Imagine the same setup on two different weeks. In a calm week, the logical stop is $6 away. In a volatile week — after a surprise Fed decision, say — the equivalent stop is $14 away. If you trade the same lot size both weeks, your real risk more than doubles without you making any conscious decision to take more risk.

That's why risk management for gold has to account for **changing volatility**, not just fixed rules.

:::note
**Pro tip:** when volatility jumps, shrink your lot size so your dollar risk stays the same. That one habit keeps you steady through the wildest gold days.
:::

## Measuring volatility simply

You don't need complex models. Common tools include:

- **Average True Range (ATR):** the average size of recent candles on a timeframe, including gaps. Many platforms show it as an indicator.
- **Daily range:** the high-to-low range of recent days compared with the past few weeks.
- **Session ranges:** how far gold has moved in the current session compared with a typical session.

The point isn't precision. It's noticing when today's environment is clearly different from normal.

## Volatility-adjusted position sizing

The core rule stays the same — **size from the stop distance** — but volatility affects where the stop must go.

:::hypo
A trader risks 1% of a $10,000 account ($100) per trade. In a calm week, the structural stop plus buffer is $6, so on a contract where $1 equals $100 per lot, size is about 0.16 lots. In a volatile week, the same structure requires a $14 stop: size falls to about 0.07 lots. Dollar risk is unchanged at around $100, but the trader now needs a larger move to reach the same profit — which is often a reason to be more selective, not to tighten the stop.
:::

## Additional rules for volatile periods

Some traders add rules that only apply when volatility is elevated, for example:

- **Reduce risk per trade** (for example from 1% to 0.5%) when the daily range is far above its recent average.
- **Fewer trades.** Volatile markets produce more apparent setups, many of them noise.
- **Wider buffers** beyond structure to account for larger wicks.
- **No entries in the first minutes** after major releases.
- **Smaller or no positions over weekends** during geopolitical stress.

## News, gaps and slippage

Volatility tends to cluster around:

- **Scheduled US data and Fed events** — read [New York-session gold trading](/strategies/new-york-session-gold-trading/).
- **Unscheduled headlines** — geopolitical events, policy surprises.
- **Market opens after weekends** — gold can open far from Friday's close.

In these moments, a stop-loss may fill well beyond its level. Your *planned* risk is not your *maximum* risk. Accounting for that is part of realistic sizing.

## Drawdown rules

Volatile markets can produce losing streaks faster than you expect. Decide in advance:

- A **daily loss limit** after which you stop.
- A **weekly or monthly drawdown** after which you reduce size or pause to review.
- A **re-entry rule** — what you must do before returning to normal size (for example, a set number of trades at reduced size within your rules).

## When not trading is the best risk management

Sometimes the right decision is to step aside:

- When you don't understand why gold is moving.
- When spreads are abnormally wide.
- When you've hit a loss limit.
- When you're making decisions emotionally.

There's no prize for trading the most volatile day of the month. The market will still be there tomorrow — the point of risk management is that you will be too.

## Track it

Add a "volatility state" field to your [trading journal](/blog/how-to-keep-a-trading-journal/) — for example calm, normal or elevated. After a few months, compare your results in each state. Many traders discover that their edge, if they have one, is concentrated in particular conditions.
